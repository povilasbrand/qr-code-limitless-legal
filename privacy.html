Publisher and contact

QR Code Limitless (the “App”) is operated by the developer identified as the publisher or seller in its app-store listing (the “Publisher”, “we”, “us”). We are responsible for personal data processed through the App and our optional support services, as described here. For privacy questions or requests, use the Give Feedback form.

This notice describes the current iOS and, where available, Android version. It is intended to explain how features actually work; your app-store purchase and device services also remain subject to Apple's or Google's separate privacy terms.

Privacy at a glance

Core QR-code and barcode scanning, creation, local History, saved items and exports are designed to work without an app account or a server-side library maintained by us. Data that you intentionally export or share can leave your device. Product and reference lookups can contact external services when Local-only mode is off.

Optional feedback and feature requests use a delivery relay. Purchases use the app store and RevenueCat. Optional technical crash reports use Sentry only after you turn that setting on. These features are explained below. Some released versions may display Google AdMob advertisements to eligible free users, subject to the app's advertising configuration and required consent.

Information kept on your device

Your scan and creation History, saved QR/barcode content, favourites, notes, folders, preferences and related app-library records are stored locally on the device. The Publisher does not operate a routine cloud account or cloud synchronization service for this local library.

Deleting items or using Settings → Privacy & Data → Reset everything & onboarding removes the relevant in-app local records. Removing the App normally removes its local app container, subject to operating-system behaviour and backups. Apple's device backups, if enabled, are controlled by your Apple settings and are not our own synchronization service.

Codes can contain private data, including Wi-Fi passwords, contact details, URLs, or payment information. Do not share sensitive exports or backups unless you understand the destination and have the required permission.

Camera, selected photos, contacts and precise location

Camera access is requested to scan codes when you use the scanner. Chosen photos or documents may be read to find and decode a QR code or barcode; selecting an image does not, by itself, upload that image to our server for decoding. The App can save an image to your library when you select export.

Contact access is used when you choose a contact to put into a code. Precise location may be requested when you choose a location-based function such as “Use current location” for a map-pin code. Permission can be refused or changed in device settings. A QR code that you later share or export can, of course, reveal information embedded in it.

If you intentionally open an external maps or website provider, that destination may receive information under its own policies.

Optional third-party product and reference lookups

When Local-only mode is off, scanning an eligible retail or reference barcode can initiate a lookup of a product code, ISBN, VIN or similar identifier through an external data source. Such a request can reveal that identifier, your IP address and ordinary connection metadata to the service that answers it. Sources can include Open Food Facts and related databases, Open Library, NHTSA and other providers available in the release.

Switching on Local-only mode disables the App's supported automatic public-data lookups while allowing core offline scanning and code creation. Links you choose to open in other apps or websites are handled by those destinations and their policies. Third-party content may be incomplete, outdated or incorrect.

App Store purchases and RevenueCat

Apple (or the applicable app marketplace) processes charges, refunds and payment credentials. We do not receive your full payment-card number. Our purchase-management service, RevenueCat, processes an App User ID generated anonymously by its SDK when no account is used, transaction and receipt details, product and entitlement status, and basic technical information such as app/platform version, locale and relevant timestamps to validate purchases, detect abuse and restore Premium access.

RevenueCat's processing occurs even if you do not opt into optional Sentry diagnostics, because it supports subscription functionality. The data is subject to RevenueCat's own retention practices and privacy terms at https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy. Purchasing does not require an account in the App.

If you explicitly choose a trial renewal reminder and grant iPhone notification permission, the App stores your reminder choice and language locally and may schedule a one-time on-device notice about a day before an active, renewing free trial ends, using the expiration date provided by RevenueCat. The reminder is not a payment service, may be delayed or blocked by iOS settings, and is not a substitute for cancelling through Apple. You can disable the reminder in the App's subscription settings. This reminder setting is not sent to our own server.

Feedback, feature ideas and code-type requests

If you voluntarily submit an app review message, feature idea or missing-code-type request, the App sends the free-text message and any email address you provide via HTTPS to a Cloudflare-hosted request service. The request service can process the connection IP address and technical request information for security, delivery and abuse prevention. The message is forwarded using an email-delivery provider to our support mailbox.

Email is optional for ordinary app feedback and feature ideas; it can be required for a specific code-type request. You do not need to send feedback to use core app features. Do not include passwords, private QR contents, or another person's personal information in a message unless necessary and lawful. We use correspondence to review requests, support users and improve the App, not to sell mailing lists. Submission is confirmed only if the relay accepts the request.

Optional Sentry crash diagnostics

Crash reporting is off by default. If you enable “Share anonymous crash reports” in Settings and Local-only mode is off, we may send technical error and crash information to Sentry to understand and repair failures. This can include error type, stack frames, app version, device model, operating system and other diagnostic context.

We configure JavaScript error handling to reduce user-entered content and disable session replay and performance sampling in the reviewed version. Native crash information and platform-generated diagnostic context cannot always be filtered by JavaScript redaction, so we cannot guarantee every diagnostic event is free of identifiers or sensitive information. We do not deliberately send decoded QR contents for analytics or advertising.

Switching off crash reporting or enabling Local-only mode stops the App's future consent-based Sentry reporting in the reviewed code. It does not automatically delete reports already received; contact us if you want us to review a deletion request. Sentry's privacy terms are available at https://sentry.io/privacy/.

Advertising consent is requested where required for storing or accessing device information and for relevant advertising processing; we do not treat a subscription purchase or general use of the App as advertising consent.

Legal obligations may require limited retention of transaction, tax, dispute or security records. Different data-protection laws may use different terminology; where the EU/UK GDPR applies, the corresponding bases may include contract (Article 6(1)(b)), legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)), consent (Article 6(1)(a)) and legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)). You can object to relevant legitimate-interest processing or withdraw consent, subject to applicable law.

Recipients and providers

Depending on the feature you use, information can be processed by Apple or Google for store and device services; Google AdMob for advertising when enabled; RevenueCat for subscriptions; Sentry for optional diagnostics; Cloudflare and an email-delivery provider for optional messages; and public product/reference services for lookups. Services reached through your own action, such as retailers, websites or maps, apply their own privacy notices.

The App includes Google AdMob for advertising. When advertising is enabled, eligible free users may see a banner directly above the Scanner tab bar or an App Open full-screen advertisement when returning to the App. Active Pro subscribers and active free-trial users are excluded, and Local-only mode suppresses ad requests. We do not provide decoded QR/barcode contents, saved codes, or local History to AdMob as ad-targeting parameters.

Retention and deletion

On-device History and created codes remain until you delete or reset them, remove the App, or your device removes the data. The Publisher does not normally possess a copy of that local library to delete remotely. Operating-system backups are managed separately.

Feedback emails and technical delivery logs may remain with our support mailbox and its service providers while reasonably needed to handle a request, prevent abuse, resolve a dispute or meet a legal duty; we have not established a universal fixed retention period for every provider's logs. RevenueCat, Apple, Sentry and third-party data sources retain records in accordance with their own service terms and applicable law.

To request access to or deletion of data we control, use the Give Feedback form and describe the relevant message or processing. We may need reasonable information to confirm a request. Some purchase records remain with the app store or are retained as legally required. Removing the App does not itself cancel a paid subscription.

Security

We use platform-provided app storage and authenticated HTTPS connections for supported online submission features, with access limited as appropriate for the services involved. No software or internet service can promise absolute security. Keep your device protected, check QR destinations before opening them, and avoid sharing codes that contain secrets.
